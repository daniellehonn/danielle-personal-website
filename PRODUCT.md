# Product

## Who it is for

A recruiter or hiring manager opening the site cold and deciding, fast,
whether Danielle is worth a conversation. Founders, engineering leaders
and design-curious peers are a welcome second audience.

## What it is

A personal site built as a publication rather than a portfolio. Danielle
Hon is an AI-native product engineer in Los Angeles: co-founder and
founding engineer at ContextForce since her junior year of high school,
now studying computer science at UCLA.

## Positioning

An AI-native product engineer who starts at the experience, not the
architecture. The work is real and shipped; the craft on the page is the
same craft she brings to a team.

## Structure

Home carries every section as one scroll, and each is also its own page:

- **cover** — the statement and where to go
- **experience** — a timeline, oldest first, so it reads as growth
- **archive** — projects the timeline does not already cover
- **about** — her own words, with photographs behind the phrases
- **project** — one artifact, told as problem, research, design,
  engineering, reflection

## Conversion

Primary is email, from the sign-off in the footer. Secondary is the
résumé, in the sidebar. Anyone not ready for either goes deeper into the
work instead.

## Voice

Precise, clever, understated. An engineer's voice: exact, unpadded, no
buzzwords, with a point of view. The copy is Danielle's own; where a
claim carries a number, the method behind it is shown.

## Design

Restrained editorial. Warm off-white, near-black ink, one cobalt accent.
Newsreader for body, Switzer for headings, IBM Plex Mono for metadata,
Phosphene for the two places a hand belongs: photo captions and the
sign-off. A drawn six-column grid, a fixed sidebar, sections numbered in
the margin.

Motion is felt rather than watched. Nothing bounces, nothing exceeds
about 0.6s, and all of it stops under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Anti-references

The template portfolio (hero photo, three cards, buzzwords). AI-generated
slop (gradient text, tracked all-caps eyebrows on every section, identical
card grids). The corporate landing page (big-metric hero, feature grids,
enterprise blue). Anything where the effects get in the way of the work.
