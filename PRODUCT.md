# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary reader is a recruiter or hiring manager opening the site cold and skimming fast for proof, impact, and fit. Founders, engineering leaders, and design-curious peers are a welcome secondary audience, but the site is optimized so a hiring reader gets what they need in the first fold. Their job to be done is simple: decide, quickly, whether Danielle is worth a conversation. The content is Danielle Hon — a Product Manager and AI Engineer at UCLA in Los Angeles — and the work leans AI-product and applied-research heavy (LEXI, RAG and PDF-extraction pipelines, model benchmarks, a multi-agent sustainability advisor).

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio whose concept is the point: one set of content rendered through several fully committed visual directions, so the medium itself argues for the maker. It exists to convert a fast skim into a conversation. Success is a hiring reader coming away convinced that Danielle is an AI engineer with genuine product taste — and reaching out.

## Positioning

An AI engineer with product taste: someone who builds intelligent tools with clarity and craft, not just models. Every screen should reinforce that the engineering is real and shipped, and that the person behind it sweats how things look and feel.

## Conversion & proof
- Primary and secondary CTA: primary is **email** (daniellehon@gmail.com); the fallback for visitors not yet ready to reach out is to **go deeper into the work** — case studies, live demos, and source.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: *builds real AI products, and makes them feel considered.*
- Belief ladder: (1) this person can actually ship engineering, not just talk about it; (2) the work spans product strategy through applied ML to the interface; (3) the craft on display here is the same craft they'd bring to my team; (4) worth an email.
- Proof on hand: concrete outcomes live in the work — an 80% reduction in scheduling time tested with 30+ students (Course Planner), an enterprise legal-tech demo shown to LegalZoom's Head of Data (PDF Extractor), and published open-source SDKs and benchmarks. No testimonials or logos supplied yet; add them to `.impeccable/assets/proof/` when available.

## Brand Personality

Precise, clever, understated. Confident craft that lets the work speak, with wit carried in the details rather than the volume. The voice is an engineer's — exact, unpadded, no buzzwords — but it has a point of view and isn't afraid to be a little playful, as the seven-direction concept shows.

## Anti-references

Explicitly not any of these: the generic template portfolio (Wix/Framer/Notion-clone hero photo, three cards, buzzwords — reads as anyone); AI-generated slop (cream backgrounds, gradient text, tracked all-caps eyebrows on every section, identical card grids — the "AI made that" tells); the corporate/SaaS landing page (big-metric hero, feature grids, enterprise blue — sells a product, not a person); and anything overdesigned or try-hard, where animation and effects get in the way of the work.

## Design Principles

- **The concept is the proof.** One content source, many committed designs — the range and rigor of the presentation is itself the argument. Don't dilute any single direction into a safe average.
- **Ship, don't claim.** Show real, deployed work and concrete outcomes over adjectives. When copy is unwritten, it stays visibly marked (`todo`) rather than shipping as filler.
- **Wit in the details, not the volume.** Cleverness shows up in a well-set label, a catalogue number, a decoder key — never in gimmicks that slow the reader down.
- **Recruiter-legible in one fold.** A fast skimmer should grasp who Danielle is and find the strongest work without scrolling far, whichever design they land on.
- **Distinctiveness over safety.** Average is invisible; each direction commits to a specific aesthetic lane rather than hedging toward neutral.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific requirements set for this project. Apply sensible web defaults where they don't fight a direction's voice — legible contrast on body copy and honoring `prefers-reduced-motion` — but none of this is a hard mandate here.
